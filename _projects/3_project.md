## **Overview**  
Climate change has significantly altered precipitation patterns and temperature, influencing the hydrology and biogeochemistry of freshwater systems. In reservoirs, these changes affect water level fluctuations, nutrient availability, and thermal stratification, ultimately shaping phytoplankton community dynamics. Lakes and reservoirs in the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River, such as Chaohu Lake, Daxi Reservoirs, and Duihekou Reservoir, provide excellent case studies for assessing the long-term ecological response of phytoplankton to climate-driven environmental changes.

## **Objective**  
These projects aimed to evaluate how increasing temperatures, precipitation, and drought conditions impact phytoplankton community composition and succession. By analyzing long-term datasets, we sought to determine the key environmental drivers of phytoplankton changes and provide guidance for reservoir management under climate change scenarios.  

## **Key Contributions**  
### 1. Analyzing Climate Trends and Reservoir Responses
   - Assessed the impact of increasing precipitation (+103%) and rising surface water temperature (+1.43°C) over 12 years.  
   - Identified key environmental shifts, including intensified thermal stratification, lower nitrogen concentrations, and phosphorus enrichment during droughts.  

### 2. Investigating the Effects of Warming and Precipitation on Phytoplankton Diversity
   - Applied a dynamic equilibrium model to show that warming and increased precipitation promote phytoplankton diversity and productivity.  
   - Identified niche complementarity and selection effects as key mechanisms driving cyanobacteria proliferation, even without significant nutrient level increases.  

### 3. Examining Drought-Induced Phytoplankton Shifts
   - Found that extreme drought conditions led to enhanced sediment resuspension, increasing turbidity and phosphorus levels.  
   - Observed a shift in dominant phytoplankton functional groups from low-turbidity taxa (L0, TD, J, X2, A) to pennate diatoms (TB), which thrive in turbid, nutrient-rich conditions.  

### 4. Providing Reservoir Management Recommendations
   - Recommended stricter phosphorus control to mitigate cyanobacteria blooms under warming conditions.  
   - Suggested adaptive water replenishment strategies to reduce the dominance of harmful algal species during droughts and improve ecological resilience.  

## **Significance**  
- **Scientific Contribution**: This study enhances our understanding of how climate change influences phytoplankton dynamics in reservoirs, emphasizing the roles of thermal stratification, nutrient shifts, and sediment resuspension.  
- **Environmental Impact**: Findings contribute to strategies for managing harmful algal blooms, which threaten water quality and aquatic biodiversity.  
- **Practical Applications**: The results support climate-adaptive reservoir management policies, focusing on nutrient control and water level regulation to maintain ecological stability.
