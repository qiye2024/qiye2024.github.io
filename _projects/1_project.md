## **Overview**
Polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) are persistent organic pollutants that pose serious environmental and health risks due to their bioaccumulation and toxicity. While traditional toxicology assumes that higher concentrations of pollutants always have more harmful effects, recent research has revealed the phenomenon of hormesis, where low doses of toxic substances may stimulate biological activity while high doses inhibit it. *Escherichia coli* (*E. coli*) is a widely used model organism in microbiological and toxicological research due to its well-characterized genetic background and ease of cultivation. Understanding how PCBs affect bacterial growth can provide insights into microbial responses to environmental pollutants, with implications for pollution risk assessment and bioremediation strategies.

## **Key Contributions**
### 1. Development of High-Throughput Screening Method
 - A microplate-based system was established to measure bacterial growth under different PCB exposure conditions efficiently.
 - Various microplate formats (12-well, 24-well, 48-well, and 96-well) were tested, and the 96-well format was optimized for its balance between throughput and accuracy.

### 2. Investigation of PCB Hormesis Effects
 - Ten PCB congeners (dichlorinated, trichlorinated, and tetrachlorinated) were tested for their effects on *E. coli* growth.
 - PCBs exhibited hormesis responses characterized by low-dose stimulation and high-dose inhibition.
 - The extent of stimulation was dependent on the chlorine substitution pattern.
### 3. Structural Dependence of PCB Hormesis Effects
 - Coplanar PCBs (e.g., PCB 12, PCB 61, PCB 77) showed weaker stimulation and stronger inhibition at high concentrations.
 - Non-Coplanar PCBs (e.g., PCB 8, PCB 10, PCB 18, PCB 28, PCB 40, PCB 52) exhibited more pronounced stimulation effects, particularly at short exposure durations.
 - Lower-chlorinated PCBs tended to induce stronger stimulation effects than higher-chlorinated ones.
### 4. Time-Dependent Effects of PCBs on E. coli
 - Hormesis effects were most significant within the first few hours of exposure.
 - Over time, PCBs exerted increasing toxic effects, leading to growth inhibition.

## **Significance**
 - **Scientific Contribution**: This study provides experimental evidence supporting the hormesis model in bacterial toxicology and highlights the structural dependence of PCB-induced effects.
 - **Environmental Implications**: Findings enhance understanding of PCB toxicity and microbial adaptation, which could aid in refining pollution risk assessments.
 - **Methodological Advancements**: The high-throughput microplate-based method can be applied to assess the hormesis effects of other environmental contaminants.
